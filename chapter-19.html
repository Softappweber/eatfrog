Strategy #19

Create Large Chunks of Time

Important work requires uninterrupted focus. Schedule it deliberately.

"You cannot eat a frog in 5-minute increments."

The Brutal Truth

Checking emails, answering texts, and scrolling social media fragment your day into tiny, useless pieces. You cannot do deep, meaningful work in 5-minute gaps.

Deep work requires long, uninterrupted blocks of time—60, 90, or 120 minutes—where you turn off your phone, close your browser tabs, and work exclusively on your biggest frog.

If you do not schedule these blocks, they will not happen. Something will always come up. Treat your time blocks like a non-negotiable appointment with yourself.

Rule: What gets scheduled gets done. What doesn't get scheduled gets lost.

Why This Works

  • Enables deep work. It takes 15–20 minutes just to reach full concentration. You need long blocks to get there.
  • Eliminates context switching. Every interruption costs you time and mental energy to refocus.
  • Creates a rhythm. Knowing you have a dedicated block reduces the urge to check your phone.
Real World Example

The Nurse: Anita works as a nurse. Her shifts are long and exhausting. But she is also studying for an advanced certification. She tries to study in 10-minute gaps between tasks, but it doesn't work. She never gets into deep focus.

Then Anita schedules a 90-minute block every evening after dinner. She puts her phone in another room. She tells her family not to disturb her. She studies with full focus for 90 minutes. In three months, she passes the certification with flying colors.

The time was always there. She just had to block it out.
