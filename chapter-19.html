Strategy #19

Create Large Chunks of Time

Important work requires uninterrupted focus. Schedule it deliberately.

"You cannot eat a frog in 5-minute increments."

The Breakdown

Checking emails, answering texts, and scrolling social media fragment your day into tiny, useless pieces. You cannot do deep, meaningful work in 5-minute gaps. You must block out large chunks of time—60, 90, or 120 minutes—where you turn off your phone, close your browser tabs, and work exclusively on your biggest frog. Treat this block of time like a non-negotiable appointment with yourself.

Why It Works

  • Enables deep work. It takes 15–20 minutes just to reach full concentration. You need long blocks to get there.
  • Eliminates context switching. Every interruption costs you time and mental energy to refocus.
  • Creates a rhythm. Knowing you have a dedicated block reduces the urge to check your phone.
Real World Example

A manager is trying to write a strategic plan. They keep trying to squeeze it in between meetings and emails. After a week, they have written nothing. They block off 9:00 AM to 11:00 AM on their calendar, put their phone in a drawer, and close their email. In two hours, they accomplish more than they did all week.

Your Turn

What is your most important task this week? Block out a 90-minute chunk tomorrow to work on it with zero distractions. When will that be?
