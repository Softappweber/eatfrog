Strategy #18

Slice and Dice the Task

Break large tasks into small, manageable pieces.

"How do you eat an elephant? One bite at a time."

The Brutal Truth

A massive project can feel like standing at the base of Mount Everest. The peak is invisible, and the climb seems impossible. So you do nothing. Not because you are lazy, but because you are overwhelmed.

The solution is not more motivation. The solution is to shrink the task. Break it into such small pieces that starting feels easy. You do not need to write a book. You just need to write one paragraph.

Rule: Focus only on the next slice. The rest will take care of itself.

Why This Works

  • It kills overwhelm. A tiny step is less frightening than a giant leap.
  • It builds momentum. Starting is the hardest part. Once you start, you keep going.
  • It creates hope. Every small completion releases dopamine, pushing you forward.
Real World Example

The Developer: Amit needs to build a mobile app for a client. It has 20 screens, user authentication, payment integration, and push notifications. He stares at the code editor and freezes. For three days, he does nothing.

Then Amit slices the task. He doesn't think about the whole app. He thinks about one screen: the login screen. He sets a timer for 10 minutes and tells himself: "I will just write the code for the login screen. That's it." Ten minutes later, the login screen is done. He feels momentum. He moves to the next screen. By the end of the week, the app is halfway done.

⏱️ The Mini-Slice Timer

Do this now. Pick one slice of a big task. Set the timer for 10 minutes. Work on that slice—and only that slice—until the timer rings.

10:00
10 minutes of focused work on ONE slice.

The Exercise

Write down the ONE slice you will work on right now, and the timer will hold you accountable.
