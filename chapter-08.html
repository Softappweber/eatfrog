Strategy #8

Prepare Thoroughly Before You Begin

Have everything you need at hand before you sit down to work.

"Preparation prevents poor performance."

The Brutal Truth

Most people start their important work in a state of chaos. They sit down to write a report, but their notes are scattered. They need a file that is on their other computer. Their phone keeps buzzing. They have to stand up five times to find things.

Every interruption breaks your focus. And focus, once lost, takes 15 to 20 minutes to rebuild. If you interrupt yourself five times, you have lost over an hour of deep work. You are not lazy. You are unprepared.

High performers prepare like pilots before takeoff. They create a pre-flight checklist. Before they start their frog, they gather all tools, files, passwords, notes, and resources. They put their phone in another room. They close unnecessary tabs. They set up their workspace so that once they begin, they do not have to stop.

Rule: The first 10 minutes of preparation save you 60 minutes of lost focus.

Why This Works

  • It removes friction. You eliminate physical and digital barriers before they happen.
  • It builds momentum. A clean, ready workspace makes it easy to dive in immediately.
  • It signals commitment. Preparing tells your brain, "We are doing this now."
  • It prevents procrastination. When everything is ready, there is no excuse to delay.
Real World Example

The Student: Ravi sits down to study for his final exams. His desk is cluttered. His books are in his bag. His laptop is at 10% battery. His phone is next to him, face-up, buzzing with notifications. He spends 20 minutes looking for his notes. Then his phone buzzes. He checks it. 45 minutes later, he has studied for exactly zero minutes.

Now Ravi prepares. The night before, he clears his desk. He charges his laptop. He puts his notes and textbooks on the desk in the exact order he will need them. He puts his phone in the kitchen. When he sits down to study, he starts within 60 seconds. He studies for 2 hours straight. Same student. Same material. Different preparation. Different result.
