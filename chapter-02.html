Strategy #2

Plan Every Day in Advance

Think on paper. Every minute you spend planning saves ten minutes in execution.

"Planning is bringing the future into the present so you can do something about it now."

The Truth About Planning

The average person wakes up and reacts. Email. Messages. Other people's emergencies. They spend the whole day putting out fires and wonder why they never move forward.

The top 10% of performers do something different. They decide the night before—or first thing in the morning—exactly what they will do, in what order, and for how long. They don't leave their day to chance. They write it down.

Rule: Never start your day until you have written down your plan.

Why This Works

  • It removes decision fatigue. When you have a written list, you don't waste mental energy choosing. You just execute.
  • It forces you to think. The act of writing separates what is truly important from what is merely noise.
  • It builds momentum. Checking off tasks creates a psychological reward that drives you forward.
  • It gives you control. You stop being a victim of your day and become the architect of it.
Real World Example

The Busy Parent: Sarah works from home while raising two kids. Every morning used to be chaos—breakfast, school runs, emails, and by 10 AM she was exhausted. Then she started planning the night before. She writes down 3 things: one work task, one home task, and one self-care task. Now she wakes up knowing exactly what matters. She does the work task while the kids eat breakfast. She does the home task during nap time. She does the self-care task after dinner. Her day is not perfect, but it is hers.

The Salesperson: Rajesh is in field sales. He used to drive around randomly, visiting whichever client was closest. He wasted hours in traffic. Now he plans his route the night before. He groups his meetings by location. He saves 90 minutes a day. That's 7.5 extra hours a week—almost a full working day—just from a simple plan.
