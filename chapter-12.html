Strategy #12

Take It One Oil Barrel at a Time

You can accomplish any task if you just take it one step at a time.

"By the yard it's hard, but inch by inch, anything's a cinch."

The Brutal Truth

Some frogs are too big to eat in one bite. You look at the whole project—write a book, build a website, launch a business—and your brain freezes. The mountain is too high. The distance is too far.

So you do nothing. You procrastinate not because you are lazy, but because you are overwhelmed.

The solution is not to find more motivation. The solution is to shrink the task. Break it into such small pieces that starting feels easy. You do not need to build a website. You just need to open the editor and write the first line of code.

Rule: Focus only on the next step. The rest will take care of itself.

Why This Works

  • It kills overwhelm. A tiny step is less frightening than a giant leap.
  • It builds momentum. Starting is the hardest part. Once you start, you keep going.
  • It creates hope. Every small completion releases dopamine, pushing you forward.
  • It makes the invisible visible. You see exactly what to do next.
Real World Example

The Writer: Kavita wants to write a book. She has wanted to write it for three years. But every time she sits down, she thinks about 300 pages. She freezes. She opens her laptop, stares at the blank page, and closes it.

Then she applies this principle. She stops thinking about the book. She thinks about one chapter. Then one page. Then one paragraph. She sets a tiny goal: "Write 100 words today." That's it.

She writes 100 words. Then 200. Then 500. The next day she writes 800. Within three months, she has a full manuscript. She didn't find a magic burst of motivation. She just took it one oil barrel at a time.
