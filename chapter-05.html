Strategy #5

Practice the ABCDE Method

Organize your list by priority before you start working. Not after.

"The first law of success is concentration: to bend all energies to one point."

The Brutal Truth

Most to-do lists are useless. They are just random collections of tasks with no hierarchy. You look at 15 items and feel overwhelmed. You do the easy ones first and avoid the hard ones. That is not productivity. That is cowardice disguised as busyness.

The ABCDE method fixes this. You assign a letter to every task:

  • A Task: Must do. Serious consequences if not done. Your frogs.
  • B Task: Should do. Mild consequences. Never do a B before all A's are done.
  • C Task: Nice to do. No consequences. Coffee with a friend. Organizing your desk.
  • D Task: Delegate. Someone else should do this. Free yourself.
  • E Task: Eliminate. It doesn't matter anymore. Delete it and never think about it again.

If you have multiple A tasks, number them: A-1, A-2, A-3. Your A-1 task is your biggest frog. Eat it first. Do not touch anything else until it is done.

Rule: Never do a B task when an A task is waiting.

Why This Works

  • It forces evaluation. You must actively decide what truly matters.
  • It removes overwhelm. You know exactly what to do next.
  • It kills busywork. C, D, and E tasks no longer steal your time.
  • It builds discipline. You train yourself to focus on A-1 before anything else.
Real World Example

The Manager: Deepak manages a team of 8 people. His typical day is chaos. He arrives at 9 AM with 12 tasks floating in his head. Emails, meetings, reports, approvals. He spends the first hour answering emails—a C task. Then he attends a meeting that could have been an email—another C task. By 5 PM, he has done nothing important.

Now Deepak uses ABCDE. He writes his list the night before. A-1: Complete the annual budget report (his boss needs it tomorrow). A-2: Approve the hiring plan for the new project. B: Respond to non-urgent emails. C: Organize his files. D: Delegate meeting notes to his assistant. E: Cancel the optional team lunch.

He arrives at 9 AM, closes his email, and works on A-1 for 90 minutes. The report is done by 10:30. The rest of the day feels light. He is in control.
