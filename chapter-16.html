Strategy #16

Practice Creative Procrastination

You can't do everything. So deliberately choose what you will NOT do.

"Procrastinate on purpose. Decide now what you will never do again."

The Brutal Truth

Everyone procrastinates. The question is: what are you procrastinating on? If you are putting off high-value tasks to do low-value busywork, you are failing.

But if you are deliberately postponing low-value tasks to focus on what truly matters, you are practicing creative procrastination. You are choosing to say no to the trivial so you can say yes to the important.

Most people never make this distinction. They treat every task as equal. They feel guilty for not doing everything. Creative procrastination removes that guilt. It gives you permission to ignore what does not matter.

Rule: You can't do everything. So choose what you won't do.

Why This Works

  • It protects your time. You stop letting trivial tasks eat your day.
  • It removes guilt. You give yourself permission to say no.
  • It sharpens focus. You force yourself to prioritize high-value work.
  • It builds boundaries. You teach others that your time is valuable.
Real World Example

The Small Shop Owner: Ramesh runs a kirana store. He spends 3 hours every day arranging products on shelves, dusting, and reorganizing inventory. He tells himself he is being productive. But these tasks do not bring in money.

Then Ramesh practices creative procrastination. He decides to stop arranging shelves daily. Instead, he does it once a week. The freed-up 3 hours per day he spends on what actually grows his business: calling suppliers to negotiate better prices, and chatting with customers to understand what they want.

Within a month, his profits increase. The shelves are a little less perfect. But the business is thriving.
